Publisher and contact

Koda Yorg; privacy inquiries: kodayorg@gmail.com.

Data used

TimeLife uses your Apple Account email, app-specific password, calendars and write permissions, event titles, dates, time zones, recurrence, locations, notes and other iCalendar fields, identifiers and ETags. Language, appearance, widget placement and startup preferences are stored locally.

Purpose and transmission

To read and modify calendars, TimeLife sends HTTPS/CalDAV requests directly to icloud.com and its subdomains, including authentication with the app-specific password. Apple's privacy documents govern its processing. This release has no developer-operated server, advertising, analytics or automatic transmission of events, passwords or diagnostics to the developer. Windows checks signatures and download reputation under its own policies. Opening Apple Account or external documents launches your browser.

Local storage and retention

The profile is %APPDATA%\Calendar U. account.enc encrypts account details with Electron safeStorage/Windows DPAPI. calendar.json and calendar.json.bak contain events and settings without app-level encryption. Temporary calendar.json.tmp and account.enc.tmp may exist during saves. Event snapshots are also held in app and worker memory. The cache persists between launches until you disconnect or delete the profile. Processes with access to your Windows profile may access it.

Deletion and control

Settings → iCloud → Disconnect removes account details and iCloud events from the app's main, backup and temporary files, clears memory caches and open editors, and preserves local events. Pending changes and conflicts must be resolved first. No server events are deleted by disconnecting. You may revoke the app-specific password in Apple Account independently.

Uninstalling preserves the profile to protect local events. To delete all app data, exit TimeLife through the tray, save any local events you need, then delete %APPDATA%\Calendar U. This deletes local events and credentials too. Ordinary file deletion is not guaranteed physical erasure; external backups and Apple's server data are managed separately.
